How we work

Capacity and weather are not negotiable

Two rules decide whether a charter operation is safe, and both are routinely bent. The first is passenger capacity: a vessel certificated for six cannot lawfully carry thirteen, whatever the occasion and however disappointed the group is.

The second is the weather call. A captain under commercial pressure to sail is the single most common factor in charters that go wrong. Agreeing a backup date at the moment of booking removes most of that pressure before it exists.

Everything else is planning. Matching draft and cruising area to what guests actually want, provisioning in advance, and thinking about parking and boarding times is what separates a good day from four hours of logistics.
